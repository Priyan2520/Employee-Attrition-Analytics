Just finished my main internship project at SyntecxHub — an HR analytics deep-dive on employee attrition using Python and Power BI. Sharing a few things I found genuinely interesting.

The dataset had 1,470 employee records with about 35 attributes — job level, salary, overtime, satisfaction ratings, age, department. The headline number was a 16.1% annual attrition rate, which is above the typical 10-12% benchmark for professional services.

A few patterns stood out:

The overtime finding was the sharpest. Employees working overtime left at 30.5% — nearly 3× the 10.4% rate for those who didn't. I was expecting it to matter, but not that much. It probably isn't overtime itself that drives people out — it's more likely a proxy for understaffed teams, poor workload planning, or roles that are harder than advertised. Either way, it's the clearest flag in the data.

Sales Representatives had a ~40% attrition rate. That's not a personality or hiring problem — it's structural. The role appears to have limited upward mobility and compensation that doesn't reflect market rates. One straightforward fix would be adding a "Senior" tier with a meaningful pay step, rather than jumping from Rep directly to Manager.

The salary gap between retained and departing employees was around $24,000 at the median. Some of that is seniority, but not all of it.

The youngest cohort (18–25) churned at 36%. That's partly expected — early-career mobility is high everywhere. But the gap from 26-35 (19.6%) to 18-25 (36.2%) is large enough to suggest the onboarding and development experience for new grads needs work.

On the technical side: built the full cleaning pipeline in Python, generated 8 charts in Matplotlib/Seaborn with a consistent corporate colour scheme, and documented the Power BI dashboard (DAX, layout, theme) in enough detail that someone could rebuild it from the guide without asking questions.

GitHub link in bio if you want to look at the code or charts.

Learned a lot about turning analysis into something a non-technical stakeholder can actually use — which turned out to be most of the work.
